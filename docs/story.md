# Story

The story is a player's career in three acts, each tied to a range of licence
tiers (see [The Exchange](exchange.md)). Free roam and contracts fill the space
between story beats, and a player can ignore the plot for as long as they like.

## Same story, personal clock

Every player plays the same story on their own real-time clock. It starts the
day they step off the drifter and stops when the season ends. Story beats are
personal, but the world they happen in is shared, so one player may be
bargaining with Receiver Hale while another is still paying off Ada. See
[Shared world](shared-world.md).

## Act 1: Passage (tiers 0–2)

1. The player steps off the drifter *Patience* at the Pads with nothing but a 2,000-mark passage debt, owned by **Ada Vellér**. Every player starts this way, owing the same creditor.
2. Day labour and off-book jobs teach traversal, trade and contracts. Each faction gets one introductory job.
3. **First real choice:** Ledger offers to buy the player's debt on gentler terms but off the books. Accept, and the player starts in the Quiet economy; refuse, and they start in the filed one.
4. The act ends when the debt is cleared and the player is licensed as a Contractor.

## Act 2: Standing (tiers 2–4)

1. The player builds a business: a stall, a warehouse, hired crews, a regular route.
2. **The thread:** Late Arrivals' filings keep disappearing from the Exchange, and their small land claims are being bought up cheaply. Registrar Kade hires the player to find out why.
3. **The reveal:** Corvane agents came in on the drifters years ago. They've been quietly buying claims so the Receivership arrives already owning half the colony.
4. The act ends when the Receivership ship enters orbit, early. Every faction now wants a Broker it can trust, or at least one it can buy.

## Act 3: The Charter (tiers 4–5)

1. Receiver Hale opens talks. The question is who counts as "the resident colony".
2. The player runs contracts for and against every faction: gathering evidence, stealing filings, moving votes, protecting the air plant.
3. **The final contract:** the player becomes Sealed and drafts the settlement. What they file decides the ending.

## Endings

| Ending | Who wins | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Title | Charter Families | Rents rise and the Fringe is fenced, but there's order and the Exchange keeps working |
| The Commons | The Crew | Air and water become public; the Families lose their estates |
| The Ledger Republic | The Registrars | The Exchange becomes a government, and every citizen holds a filed share |
| Open Sky | The Quiet Book | The charter is burned, the Exchange falls and nothing is enforced |
| The Sale | Corvane | The player gets very rich and the planet becomes a company asset again |
| The Late Claim *(hidden)* | The Late Arrivals | Only if the Late Arrivals were organised in Act 2; the newest people inherit the planet |

## Endings in a shared world

Each player's final filing decides *their* ending: an epilogue, their standing
with each faction, and what becomes of their property. The shared world doesn't
flip every time a player finishes.

Instead, at the end of each three-month season every filing is tallied, and the
winning settlement shapes the next season's world. Then the world resets, and a
share of the season's whole economy goes to the richest player by net worth,
whatever their tier.
