# Look and feel

Earth Two looks like a frontier town built from the parts of a spaceship:
patched, repainted and lived in. Realistic people in a familiar but slightly
wrong world.

## Sky and light

The air is thin and dusty, so the sky is pale ochre by day and violet at dusk.
The sun is TRAPPIST-1, a red dwarf: orange-red, and four times the size the
Sun looks from Earth, because the Red orbits so close to it. It hangs low over
Landfall. Beside it the Red's neighbour planets show as discs along the sky,
dark when they're between the Red and the sun, lit when they're beyond it. The light inside the domes is filtered and
warm. Indoors in the Hull it's sodium-orange work lamps and cold blue status
lights. Dust storms are the main weather and should turn the lighting brown and
close.

## Materials

Ship alloy that's been scratched, welded and painted over in many colours. Dome
glass, fogged at the edges. Local concrete poured from the planet's red soil.
Fabric everywhere: awnings, curtains, rugs and tarps, because cloth is cheap and
metal isn't.

## Faction looks

Each faction reads at a glance (see [Factions](factions.md)):

- **Charter Families:** old company white and navy, kept clean. The only straight lines in town.
- **The Crew:** utility orange and grey, tools on belts, numbers stencilled on everything.
- **Registrars:** plain dark coats and a brass seal at the collar.
- **Quiet Book:** nothing in common, on purpose.
- **Fringers:** sun-bleached layers, masks and rebreathers, red dust on everything.
- **Corvane:** clean, new and pressed. They look wrong here, and that's the point.

## Asset strategy

Since the world is "real objects, retrofitted", real-world props and textures
(Poly Haven and similar) can be reused: crates, pipes, scaffolding, market
stalls, concrete. Making them sci-fi is mostly a matter of kitbashing,
repainting and adding a few signature pieces: dome frames, ship hull plates,
rebreathers, filter cartridges and terminal kiosks.

## Sound

Air handlers humming everywhere in the Hull. Wind and grit outside. Market
chatter in a mix of accents (it's a colony of many origins). The Exchange's
floor bell rings when a big contract is filed.

## UI

Square corners on every panel, button and keycap, never rounded. Contract
paperwork is the UI's motif: menus look like filed forms, stamps confirm
actions, and Registrar seals mark licence tiers.
