# Economy

Everything can be owned and sold, contracts and debts included. Prices are set
per district by supply and demand, and world events push them around. Arbitrage
between districts is the basic way to make money from trade. The world's
economy is shared by every player and keeps running while they're offline.

## Money

There are three currencies, and switching between them is gameplay in itself.

- **Marks.** Exchange credit, backed by filed contracts. Used for everything legal.
- **Filters.** Air-filter cartridges. The Fringe's real currency, and steady in value because everyone breathes.
- **Corvane scrip.** Old company money, worthless for 50 years. If the Receivership honours it in Act 3 it's worth a fortune. Speculating on scrip is a long bet a player can make from day one.

## What's traded

| Category | Examples | Why it's scarce | Cheap in | Dear in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Air and water | Filter cartridges, rebreathers, water rights | The Crew controls supply | The Hull | The Fringe |
| Food | Dome crops, Fringe grain, protein vat output, real Earth coffee | Domes are small; Earth goods are rare | The Fringe | Charter Row |
| Parts | Ship salvage, terraformer components, power cells | Nobody can make precision parts any more | The Fringe (salvage) | The Hull (repairs) |
| Off-world goods | Medicine, seeds, electronics, luxuries | Only drifters bring them | The Pads, on the day a drifter lands | Everywhere else |
| Weapons | Earth guns and ammo, laser guns, katanas and other blades, stun batons, armour | Earth guns and ammo come only on drifters; lasers burn power cells | The Pads, on drifter days; the Quiet Book, unfiled | The Fringe |
| Contraband | Unfiled weapons, forged seals, Corvane data cores | Banned by one faction or another | The Quiet Book | Whoever wants it |
| Paper | Contracts, debts, land claims, filed rumours | It's what decides who owns what | The Exchange floor | Whoever is losing a dispute |
| Property | Stalls, rooms, warehouses, haulers, bikes | Space under the domes is limited | The Fringe | Charter Row |
| Slots | A place in a full world | The population is capped | A walked-out player's open slot | An auction on the Exchange |

Weapons are anything money can buy, and fights can be lethal. Vehicles must be
earned, either bought or given as a gift.

## Events that move prices

- A **drifter lands**: off-world goods crash at the Pads for a day, and labour gets cheap because new arrivals need work.
- A **dust storm**: filters double in price and Fringe trade stops.
- An **air-plant fault**: the Crew's leverage spikes and everything in the Hull costs more.
- **Charter news** in Act 3: land, scrip and paper swing wildly on every rumour.

## Trading paper

Trading paper is what makes "trade anything" deep. A player can buy someone's
debt and collect on it, sell a contract they can't finish, or buy a land claim
and wait for the charter to settle. Players can buy and sell each other's debts
too, so a rival's debt is a lever as well as an asset.

## Slots and walking out

A player can sell their slot to a newcomer, either by auction or by direct sale,
and the buyer inherits everything except the avatar's name and look. A player
who walks out for good has their property auctioned, and whoever takes the open
slot inherits whatever is still unsold. Full rules are in
[Shared world](shared-world.md).

## The season payout

When a season ends and the world resets, a share of the season's whole economy
goes to the richest player by net worth. See [Shared world](shared-world.md).
