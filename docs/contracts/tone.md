# Voice and form

The job is a promise someone wants written down, or deliberately left off the
books. Its immediate problem should be understandable before its politics.
Paper, air, labour, land and passage debts give the problems their weight.

## At the terminal

A full offer has a plain title, a short message from the issuer, then the
actual terms. The message can expose a motive or a disagreement. The fields
must still say exactly what the player delivers, where, when, for what pay
and with what liability. The player should not have to decode a joke to learn
the penalty.

Keep the issuer message to roughly two or three sentences. Use concrete
objects: a valve, six cases, the unsigned page. Save a faction's full argument
for the work and its consequences. An ordinary contract should sound worth
doing even to someone who has never read the world bible.

The journal's available-contract notices contain only terminal, location and
count. The messages in the tier sheets below are **terminal copy**, not text
to put on the money card or in the notification list.

## The pressure in the paper

Low-tier work asks for a small thing and reveals who gets paid for it. Higher
tiers give the player responsibility for wages, another person's claim or a
faction's authority. The language can stay plain as the stakes rise.

Legal work can be cruel. Off-book work can be helpful. A medical delivery can
still make the player answerable to Ledger; a correctly filed rent collection
can still put somebody out of a room. Show the competing interests rather
than awarding a moral label in the offer text.

Each repeat changes an actual assignment. A fresh crate number, fault report
or claimant matters more than rewriting every sentence for variety. Unique
instances should feel like different work, not identical people all promised
the one package lying on a desk.

## Reading the drafts

The quoted issuer messages are proposed game copy. The work and terms beneath
them are design specifications for review. Pay and deadlines are provisional;
the clear outcome and the speaker's motive are the parts to judge first.

Review for a believable request, a distinctive speaker, a deliverable outcome
and a consequence that follows from the paper. The player is an arrival
looking for work, not someone every stranger has already decided will save
the colony.
